Where did Australia's tech jobs go?

Job ads for ICT professionals (developers, programmers, analysts and security specialists) have fallen to less than half of their January 2019 level. This page shows where it happened, how fast, and how tech compares with the rest of the job market.

−49% ICT job ads in August 2026 compared with January 2019
−61% below the April 2022 peak of about 14,800 ads a month
1 of 48 ICT had the biggest fall of any of 48 occupation groups

Good to know

  • Job ads are the online job advertisements counted each month by Jobs and Skills Australia. They show how much employers want to hire, not how many people already work in a job.
  • ICT Professionals is the official occupation group for software and web developers, programmers, analysts, and database, network and security specialists.
  • Share of job ads means ICT ads as a percentage of all job ads in the same place, which lets us compare big and small states fairly.

1. The boom and the bust

Tech hiring has always moved in waves. Ads hit their highest point in the 20-year record in February 2008, dropped sharply during the 2020 COVID-19 lockdowns, then surged to a post-2019 high in April 2022. Since then they have fallen steadily. In August 2026 there were about 5,800 ICT job ads a month, roughly 61% below that peak.

−61%from the April 2022 peak to August 2026

How to read this

Each point on the line is one month. The higher the line, the more ICT jobs employers were advertising. The labelled dots mark the two peaks, the COVID-19 low and the latest month.

What stands out

The 2022 rebound was big (from about 7,000 to nearly 14,800 ads), but it was followed by three years of steady decline. The latest month is lower than the COVID-19 low.

Was this just a weak job market? No. Counting all other occupations together, job ads in August 2026 were about 22% higher than in January 2019. ICT ads started level with them, rose just as fast in 2021–22, then fell away from mid-2022 onwards and ended 49% below where they began.

−49%ICT job ads since January 2019, against +22% for all other jobs

How to read this

Both lines start at 100, which is the number of ads in January 2019. A line above 100 means more ads than then; below 100 means fewer.

What stands out

The two lines move together until 2022, so the pandemic hit and rebound were shared. After that, only ICT keeps falling. The gap between the lines is about 70 points.

2. A nationwide slump, but not an even one

ICT roles make up a smaller slice of all job ads in every state than they did in January 2019. The ACT stands out: about one in ten of its job ads (10.3%) is still for an ICT professional, far above New South Wales and Victoria (about 3.2% and 3.3%) and well above every other state.

10.3%of ACT job ads are for ICT, the highest share in Australia

How to read this

Each state is shaded by the share of its job ads that are for ICT professionals. A darker colour means ICT is a bigger part of the local job market. Numbers on the map give the exact share.

What stands out

The ACT (red ring) is the only place in the darkest group, at 10.3%. Every other state is under 3.5%. Big states are not necessarily tech-heavy: Queensland and Western Australia are both under 2%.

The biggest cities, where most tech work is advertised, lost the most. Sydney is down 53% since January 2019, Melbourne 48% and Canberra 45%. Hobart is the only capital with more ICT ads than in 2019 (+6%), but that comes from a very small base of about 36 ads a month.

−53%Sydney, the biggest tech job market, since January 2019

How to read this

The size of each circle shows how many ICT ads a city has today. The colour shows how much that number has changed since January 2019: dark red is a big fall, pale is little change, blue is growth.

What stands out

Sydney and Melbourne are the biggest circles and also among the darkest, so the largest tech markets lost the most ads. Adelaide (−14%) and Darwin (−6%) held up best among the cities that fell.

The next map shrinks and grows each state to match its number of ICT ads, so that big job markets are easy to compare. In 2019 the ACT advertised more ICT jobs than Western Australia or South Australia, and it still does (about 460 ads a month against 440 and 300). The ACT had 47% fewer than before; Tasmania (+8%) and the Northern Territory (+1%) are the only places not down, but both advertise only about 48 and 35 ICT jobs a month.

What stands out

NSW and Victoria have the biggest rings, and their circles have shrunk the most. Tasmania and the Northern Territory are tiny, with ring and circle almost the same size.

Today it has 35%, while Queensland, Western Australia and South Australia each hold a bigger slice than before.

3. Tech compared with the rest of the job market

Most kinds of work are advertised more than before the pandemic. Only 11 of the 48 occupation groups have fewer ads than in January 2019, and ICT fell more than twice as far as the next worst (Transport and Design Professionals, and Architects, −23%). Medical practitioners and nurses more than doubled (+123%).

1 of 48occupation groups: ICT fell the most

How to read this

Each row is one group of jobs. A dot to the right of the vertical line means more ads than in January 2019; a dot to the left means fewer. The longer the line, the bigger the change.

What stands out

Almost everything sits to the right. ICT (red, bottom) is the only group below −25%. The top of the list is mostly health and trades work.

The fall is not just a story about small occupations. In January 2019 ICT was the second-biggest of the 48 groups, with about 11,400 ads a month. Of the 14 groups that advertised 5,000 or more jobs a month then, half have grown since. The biggest group of all, General-Inquiry Clerks and Call Centre Workers, is almost unchanged (−2%).

2nd of 48ICT's rank by job ads in January 2019, before the biggest fall of any group

How to read this

Each dot is one group of jobs. The further right, the more job ads the group had in January 2019. The higher up, the more it has grown since. Dots below the black line have fewer ads than in 2019.

What stands out

ICT (red) is alone in the bottom right: a big group with the deepest fall. The next-worst of the large groups (Business, Finance and Human Resource Professionals) is down 21%, less than half as far.

4. Where tech is still a big slice

Tech's share of job ads is lower in every state than it was in 2019, and it has fallen steadily in most. The ACT peaked at 19.0% in 2020 and was still 10.9% in 2026. New South Wales and Victoria went from around 6–7% of ads in 2019–21 to 3.5% in 2026.

19.0%the ACT's peak ICT share of job ads, in 2020

How to read this

Each row is a state and each column a year. The darker the cell, the larger the ICT share of that state's job ads, and the number in the cell gives the exact percentage.

What stands out

The ACT row stays dark throughout. Rows for NSW, Victoria and Western Australia fade from dark to light, so the shift happens everywhere, not just in one place.

Over the full 20-year record, the ACT has had the highest ICT share of any state in every one of the 248 months. That lead is shrinking: its share has dropped from a peak of 24.4% in August 2006 to 10.3% now. Across Australia, ICT's share of all job ads is now 2.7%, the lowest of the whole record.

How to read this

What stands out

The red line is far above all the others at every point, but it has fallen the furthest in percentage points. The grey lines bunch together at the bottom, and most of them are lower now than they were in 2019.

The capital cities tell the same story. Every city's ICT share is lower than in January 2019. Canberra is still far ahead (10.3%), though it started from about 16%. Sydney's share almost halved, from 8.3% to 4.5%.

5. Fewer ads, but the tech workforce kept growing

Fewer job ads do not mean fewer tech workers. The 2021 Census counted over 260,000 ICT professionals and 74,000 ICT managers, about 86,000 more than in 2016. Every ICT occupation grew, led by ICT Support and Test Engineers (+54%). Job ads measure new hiring, so hiring can cool even while a large workforce stays in place. The Census is the most recent official count of workers by occupation.

+54%growth in ICT Support and Test Engineers, 2016 to 2021

How to read this

Each bar is one ICT occupation. The longer the bar, the more the number of workers grew between the 2016 and 2021 Censuses.

What stands out

All five bars point the same way: ICT employment was still growing in 2021, before the ad slump. This is the earlier period, so it cannot show what has happened since.

6. Employers also say fewer tech jobs are hard to fill

Job ads are not the only signal. Each year, Jobs and Skills Australia rates occupations as in "shortage" when employers cannot find enough qualified people. Of the 43 ICT occupations on the list, 23 were rated in shortage in 2022. By 2026 that had fallen to 8. The jobs still in shortage are specialist ones. Cloud Engineer, DevOps Engineer, Cyber Security Architect and Cyber Governance, Risk and Compliance Specialist have been rated in shortage in all six years.

23 → 8of 43 ICT occupations rated in shortage, 2022 to 2026

How to read this

Each row is an ICT job and each column is a year. A red square means the job was rated in national shortage that year, so employers struggled to find enough qualified people. Grey means no shortage.

What stands out

The red area shrinks from left to right. Software Engineer was rated in shortage from 2021 to 2024 but not in 2025 or 2026. The 8 jobs in shortage in 2026 are specialists in cyber security, cloud, DevOps and data. So fewer job ads has not made every tech job easy to fill. The list does not say why.

What it adds up to

Tech hiring has cooled sharply since its 2022 peak. It fell further than any other occupation group, in nearly every state and capital city, while the wider job market stayed strong. Fewer ICT jobs are rated in shortage than in 2022, though specialist roles in cyber security, cloud and data are still hard to fill. The data cannot tell us why. It shows that employers advertise far fewer tech roles than they did before the pandemic. Whether this is a temporary reset or a longer-term change is the question to watch.

  • Job ads: Jobs and Skills Australia, Internet Vacancy Index, August 2026 release: "ANZSCO2 Occupations, States and Territories" (Figures 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11) and "ANZSCO2 Occupations, GCCSA and SA4 Regions" (Figures 4 and 12). The index counts online job ads from Seek, CareerOne and Workforce Australia. Figures 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11 use JSA's trend series; Figures 4 and 12 use 3-month moving averages.
  • Workforce: Australian Bureau of Statistics, 2021 Census data shows Australia going high tech, 12 October 2022 (Figure 13). The ABS reports Software and Applications Programmers as "up 47 per cent".
  • Shortages: Jobs and Skills Australia, Occupation Shortage List, 2021 to 2026 releases (Figure 14). Forty-three ICT occupations were picked from the full lists by OSCA code: ICT managers (113), ICT professionals including cyber security, software and cloud (27), ICT and telecommunications technicians (314) and the four data roles (2232). ICT sales and business development roles are not included. Four of the 43 were not rated in 2021 and are not counted as shortages that year.
  • Maps: State boundaries from rowanhogan/australian-states (based on ABS boundaries), simplified for fast loading and drawn with an equal-area conic projection. Capital-city positions are approximate. In Figure 5 the circles are moved slightly from each state's true position so they do not overlap, so positions are only roughly geographic.
  • Method: ICT Professionals is ANZSCO major group 26. "Share of job ads" is ICT ads divided by all ads in the same state or city. In Figure 2, "all other occupations" is all job ads minus ICT ads; each line is divided by its own January 2019 value. In Figure 7, each state's ICT ads are divided by the total of the eight states' ICT ads (the states' totals differ slightly from the national figure). Annual shares in Figure 10 are the sum of ICT ads divided by the sum of all ads across the months in each year.
  • Tools: Charts are built with Vega-Lite; each chart's specification is in the specs/ folder of this repository.
